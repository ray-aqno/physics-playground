## LAB · Units and dimensional analysis

`lab.units` · hash `d7e16ae7` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.units.q1** (multiple choice): A pendulum's period T (seconds) might depend on its length L (m) and g (m/s²). Which formula has the right dimensions?
  - **Key:** T = 2π √(L / g)
  - Why: L/g has units m ÷ (m/s²) = s², and its square root is s. The other choices give 1/s, m²/s² and s².
- **lab.units.q2** (numeric): Convert a highway speed of 72 km/h to m/s.
  - **Key:** 20 m·s^-1
  - Worked: 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 72000 / 3600 m/s = 20 m/s.
- **lab.units.q3** (numeric): What is the momentum of a 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s? (kg·m/s or N·s are both fine.)
  - **Key:** 30000 kg·m·s^-1
  - Worked: p = (1500 kg)(20 m/s) = 30,000 kg·m/s = 3.0 × 10⁴ N·s.
- **lab.units.q4** (TRIAGE): The air drag force on a fast-moving object depends on the air density ρ (kg/m³), the speed v (m/s) and the front area A (m²): F = C ρ^a v^b A^c, where C has no units. Use dimensional analysis to find the power b on the speed.
  - **Key:** 2 (no units)
  - Worked: Only F (s⁻²) and v (s⁻¹) contain seconds, so s⁻² = (s⁻¹)^b gives b = 2. Drag grows with the square of speed.
  - Identify check: Same dimensions on both sides
- **lab.units.q5** (explain it simply): Explain how checking units can tell you an answer is wrong, but can never prove it is right.
  - Model answer: If the units do not match, the formula cannot be right, so a units mismatch proves an error. But a formula can have perfect units and still be off by a number like 2 or π, or use the wrong physics. Matching units is necessary, not sufficient.

## LAB · Significant figures and estimates

`lab.sigfigs` · hash `052d5a22` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.sigfigs.q1** (multiple choice): How many significant figures does 0.00340 have?
  - **Key:** 3
  - Why: The significant digits are 3, 4 and the final 0: three significant figures.
- **lab.sigfigs.q2** (numeric, tolerance 3%): A cart rolls 2.4 m in 1.37 s. What is its average speed, to the right number of significant figures?
  - **Key:** 1.7518 m·s^-1
  - Worked: v = 2.4 m / 1.37 s = 1.75 m/s, which rounds to 1.8 m/s (two significant figures).
- **lab.sigfigs.q3** (numeric, tolerance 10%): Estimate the mass of air in a classroom 4 m × 5 m × 2.5 m. Air has a density of about 1.2 kg/m³.
  - **Key:** 60 kg
  - Worked: Volume = 4 × 5 × 2.5 = 50 m³. Mass = 1.2 kg/m³ × 50 m³ = 60 kg, about the mass of a person.
- **lab.sigfigs.q4** (explain it simply): Why is writing "1.751824818 m/s" for the cart's speed worse than writing "1.8 m/s", even though it has more digits?
  - Model answer: The extra digits claim a precision the measurements never had. The distance was only known to about 0.1 m, so the speed is only known to about 0.1 m/s. Writing more digits misleads the reader about how well we know the answer.

## LAB · Right triangles and resolving vectors

`lab.trig` · hash `0017b7c0` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.trig.q1** (numeric): A rope pulls a sled with a 50 N force at 30° above the horizontal. What is the horizontal component of the force?
  - **Key:** 43.301 kg·m·s^-2
  - Worked: F_x = F cos θ = (50 N)(cos 30°) = (50 N)(0.8660) = 43.3 N.
- **lab.trig.q2** (numeric): Same rope, same 50 N at 30° above horizontal. What is the vertical component?
  - **Key:** 25 kg·m·s^-2
  - Worked: F_y = F sin θ = (50 N)(sin 30°) = (50 N)(0.5) = 25 N.
- **lab.trig.q3** (numeric): A ramp rises 1.5 m over a horizontal distance of 4.0 m. What angle does it make with the ground? (Answer in degrees.)
  - **Key:** 20.556°
  - Worked: tan θ = rise / run = 1.5 / 4.0 = 0.375, so θ = arctan(0.375) = 20.6°.
- **lab.trig.q4** (multiple choice): A force points 70° above the horizontal. Which component is larger?
  - **Key:** Vertical
  - Why: At 70° the vector is close to vertical, so sin 70° = 0.94 is much bigger than cos 70° = 0.34. They are equal only at 45°.
- **lab.trig.q5** (explain it simply): In your own words: why does the x-component use cosine when the angle is measured from the x-axis? What changes if the angle is measured from the y-axis instead?
  - Model answer: Cosine gives the side next to the angle. When the angle is measured from the x-axis, the side next to it lies along x, so x gets cos θ. If the angle is measured from the y-axis, the side next to it lies along y, so then y gets cos θ and x gets sin θ. The rule is "next to the angle gets cosine", not "x always gets cosine".

## LAB · Components, magnitude and direction

`lab.vectors` · hash `3d1a9220` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.vectors.q1** (numeric): A puck has velocity ⟨-6, 8⟩ m/s. What is its speed?
  - **Key:** 10 m·s^-1
  - Worked: |v| = √((-6)² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 m/s.
- **lab.vectors.q2** (multiple choice): For v = ⟨-6, 8⟩ m/s, a calculator gives arctan(8 / -6) = -53.1°. What is the real direction, measured counterclockwise from +x?
  - **Key:** 126.9°
  - Why: Left and up is the second quadrant (between 90° and 180°). The calculator answer is off by 180°: -53.1° + 180° = 126.9°.
- **lab.vectors.q3** (numeric): Find the direction of ⟨-6, 8⟩ m/s in degrees, counterclockwise from +x.
  - **Key:** 126.87°
  - Worked: Reference angle = arctan(8/6) = 53.13°. The vector points left and up, so θ = 180° - 53.13° = 126.87°.
- **lab.vectors.q4** (numeric): A displacement of 12 m points at 210° (counterclockwise from +x). Give it as a vector ⟨x, y⟩ in meters.
  - **Key:** ⟨-10.392, -6⟩ m
  - Worked: x = 12 cos 210° = -12 cos 30° = -10.39 m; y = 12 sin 210° = -12 sin 30° = -6.0 m. So ⟨-10.4, -6.0⟩ m.
- **lab.vectors.q5** (explain it simply): Explain why you cannot always trust the calculator's arctan to give a vector's direction, and how you fix it.
  - Model answer: arctan only knows the ratio a_y/a_x, and ⟨-6, 8⟩ and ⟨6, -8⟩ have the same ratio, so it cannot tell which way the vector points. It always answers between -90° and 90°. Look at the signs of the components to find the quadrant; if a_x is negative, add 180°.

## LAB · Adding and subtracting vectors

`lab.vector-add` · hash `66e3adce` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.vector-add.q1** (numeric): A = ⟨3, 4⟩ m and B = ⟨2, -7⟩ m. Find A + B.
  - **Key:** ⟨5, -3⟩ m
  - Worked: A + B = ⟨3 + 2, 4 + (-7)⟩ = ⟨5, -3⟩ m.
- **lab.vector-add.q2** (numeric): With the same A = ⟨3, 4⟩ m and B = ⟨2, -7⟩ m, find A - B.
  - **Key:** ⟨1, 11⟩ m
  - Worked: A - B = ⟨3 - 2, 4 - (-7)⟩ = ⟨1, 11⟩ m.
- **lab.vector-add.q3** (TRIAGE): A hiker walks 3.0 km due east, then 4.0 km in a direction 30° north of west. How far is the hiker from the starting point?
  - **Key:** 2053.2 m
  - Worked: x: 3.0 + (-4.0 cos 30°) = 3.0 - 3.46 = -0.46 km. y: 0 + 4.0 sin 30° = 2.0 km. Distance = √(0.46² + 2.0²) = √4.215 = 2.05 km.
  - Translate check: Size of the total displacement
- **lab.vector-add.q4** (explain it simply): A friend says "3 km plus 4 km is always 7 km". When are they right, and when are they wrong?
  - Model answer: They are right only when both displacements point the same way. Displacements add tip to tail, so if they point in different directions they partly cancel or add at an angle. The result can be anywhere from 1 km (opposite directions) to 7 km (same direction).

## LAB · The dot product

`lab.dot` · hash `06e30873` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.dot.q1** (numeric): Compute ⟨3, 4⟩ · ⟨4, -3⟩.
  - **Key:** 0 (no units)
  - Worked: (3)(4) + (4)(-3) = 12 - 12 = 0. The vectors are perpendicular.
- **lab.dot.q2** (numeric): A force F = ⟨10, 5⟩ N pushes a box through a displacement d = ⟨3, 0⟩ m. How much work does the force do?
  - **Key:** 30 kg·m^2·s^-2
  - Worked: W = (10 N)(3 m) + (5 N)(0 m) = 30 J. Only the part of F along the motion does work.
- **lab.dot.q3** (numeric): What is the angle between ⟨2, 1⟩ and ⟨1, 3⟩? (Degrees.)
  - **Key:** 45.000°
  - Worked: A · B = 2 + 3 = 5. |A| = √5, |B| = √10. cos θ = 5/√50 = 0.7071, so θ = 45°.
- **lab.dot.q4** (multiple choice): A friction force points opposite to a box's motion. What is the sign of the work it does?
  - **Key:** Negative
  - Why: cos 180° = -1, so W = F · d is negative. Friction takes kinetic energy away.
- **lab.dot.q5** (explain it simply): Explain why a force perpendicular to an object's motion does no work on it.
  - Model answer: Work is F · d, which only counts the part of the force along the motion. A perpendicular force has no part along the motion (cos 90° = 0), so it can change the direction of the motion but not the speed.

## LAB · The cross product

`lab.cross` · hash `251d7c9c` · **awaiting review**

- **lab.cross.q1** (numeric): Find the z-component of ⟨3, 0⟩ × ⟨0, 2⟩.
  - **Key:** 6 (no units)
  - Worked: (3)(2) - (0)(0) = 6. Going from +x to +y is counterclockwise, so the result is positive.
- **lab.cross.q2** (numeric): A wrench is held at r = ⟨0.20, 0⟩ m from a bolt, and you push with F = ⟨0, -50⟩ N. Find the z-component of the torque τ = r × F in N·m.
  - **Key:** -10 kg·m^2·s^-2
  - Worked: τ_z = (0.20)(-50) - (0)(0) = -10 N·m. Negative means clockwise when viewed from +z.
- **lab.cross.q3** (numeric): Compute ⟨1, 2, 3⟩ × ⟨4, 5, 6⟩ as a 3-D vector.
  - **Key:** ⟨-3, 6, -3⟩ (no units)
  - Worked: x: 2·6 - 3·5 = -3. y: 3·4 - 1·6 = 6. z: 1·5 - 2·4 = -3. Result ⟨-3, 6, -3⟩.
- **lab.cross.q4** (multiple choice): If A × B = ⟨0, 0, 5⟩, what is B × A?
  - **Key:** ⟨0, 0, -5⟩
  - Why: The cross product is anti-commutative: B × A = -(A × B) = ⟨0, 0, -5⟩.
- **lab.cross.q5** (explain it simply): Explain why pushing straight toward a door's hinge cannot open the door, using the cross product.
  - Model answer: Torque is r × F, with size |r| |F| sin θ. Pushing straight toward the hinge makes the force parallel to r, so θ = 0 and sin θ = 0. There is no torque, so the door does not start to rotate however hard you push.
